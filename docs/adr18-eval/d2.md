Branch `wip/adr18-d2`, based on master 237b7c4. Nothing is merged, pushed or

## Verdict in one paragraph

**(d2) works where (a) failed: under Thread, and on an R1 pair.** Under Thread
(every extension refused) all four d2 runs booked, matched and granted every
tracked slot with full notice, lost none, and kept tracked loss within
master's against the same reference (5.6-9.7 % against 6.6-7.6 %, Garmin
0-0.4 %), while the scan heard 24-25 of 25 phase bins against master's 7 and
found an in-band master in 4 of 6 trials against master's 0 of 6. On an R1
pair walked to 12.5 ms both channels kept their slots on air (A 217, B 218
frames against master's A 221, B 0). On `gate.conf` the asserted deaf
fraction falls from 30/40/80/100 % to **6.1/9.5/13.4/26.9 %** at 1/2/4/8
tracked channels, and on air in-band discovery went from 1 of 5 to 4 of 5.
Under BLE (1 s advertiser) it behaves the same way: every phase heard, in-band
4 of 6 against master's 1 of 3, and the only tracked slots lost were bookings
the advertiser's own events refused (2 and 6 per 120 s, as a tracked request
is refused today). It took seven on-air findings to get there; one matters
beyond d2: under Thread **MPSL ASSERT 69:108** follows extension refusals
(2 of 2 first d2 runs halted on it; master and (a) have hit it on the same
path), and stopped once the gate stopped asking for extensions the arbiter
keeps refusing (0 in 2 runs of ~9000 placements). What d2 does not close is
the **first ~9.5 ms after each tracked window** (the window's follow-on
reserve, which the backend never releases early once the window has received
a frame, then the elastic lead), so a master sitting just after a tracked slot
is still missed; (a) closed that band where it worked. Costs: about +4.9 kB
flash and +640 B RAM on every MPSL image (the L15 Matter image keeps 8.2 kB of
RAM), one more MPSL session per app, and no change to the HAL's
one-operation rule. Zero cost on plain builds: PASS, all eight rows byte-identical. **Verdict: (d2)
passes the kill criterion under Thread, under BLE, on `gate.conf` and on an R1
pair, and is the candidate to carry into Phase 2**, with the extension skip
(it is what makes it stable under Thread) and, as a separate fix, the early
release for frame-bearing tracked windows (master's code; it would shrink the
after-slot band to ~5.5 ms). It composes with (e) (the elastic lead is what
(e) would tune) and replaces (a) at the tracked boundary.

## Design

### What the second session is for

With one session, every grant boundary is a full placement lead with the radio
idle, because `mpsl_timeslot_request()` answers `-NRF_EAGAIN` unless the
session is IDLE. A second session is a second request slot: a request can sit
with MPSL, placed with full notice, while the other session's grant is live.

Phase 0c F2 says how MPSL treats two of our sessions: first come, first served,
priority irrelevant. A request placed before the other session's grant covers
its instant is granted every time, and the live grant's next extension is
refused 0.2-0.4 ms before it (layout A). A request placed into air the live
grant already holds is BLOCKED every time (layout B).

F1 and (a) say what a Thread build does: every extension is refused and every
request placed with 24 ms of notice is granted. So under Thread the only thing
that works is a **fresh request placed a full lead ahead**, and the only thing
that never works is an extension. (d2) is built so that a tracked slot depends
on the first and never on the second.

### The rule: book the committed operation, a lead ahead, in the other session

The gate keeps **a pool of two sessions**. Neither is "the tracked session":
any request goes into whichever session is free. What changes is that the core
may hand the gate a **booking**: "the committed operation at [t_from, t_to]
will be armed later; place its request now". The booking is placed at once,
into a free session, with whatever notice is left (normally far more than a
lead). The scan chunk in front of it then runs in the other session up to a
small gap before the booked instant, instead of stopping a whole lead before
it. When the chunk ends, the core arms the committed operation, and the gate
finds it inside the booking and stages it behind the request already placed.

This is F2's layout A by construction:

- The booking is placed **before** the scan's grant can reach the booked
  instant. The scan's request asks for at most its first bite (10 ms, never
  past its own close), and the extension chain grows just in time, 1 ms at a
  compare 2 ms before the end. The scheduler books the committed operation
  **before** it arms the scan chunk in front of it, so the booking is always
  first come.
- The scan chunk's close is placed by the scheduler (`D2_GAP_US` before the
  booked open, plus the frame tail, dispatch slack and in-grant lead), so the
  chunk normally ends as an operation on its own close. If its last extension
  would overlap the booking, MPSL refuses it, and the chunk ends ABORTED a
  millisecond or two early. Either way it costs listening only.
- A tracked slot therefore depends on **one fresh request with full notice**,
  the thing Thread grants, and on nothing the scan does.

### One operation at a time is kept

The HAL's one-operation rule is not broken. A booking is not an operation: it
has no op id, no terminal and no callback, and the core arms the booked
operation in the ordinary way when its turn comes. At most one operation is
armed, staged behind at most one placed request. The gate holds at most one
extra placed request (the booking), and MPSL itself guarantees our two
sessions' timeslots never overlap, so the radio still has one owner and one
live grant.

### Refusal paths, and what each may cost

| Path | Gate | Core | Slot lost? |
|---|---|---|---|
| booking BLOCKED (answered at placement, F2/F3: ~28 us after the request) | session marked failed, window kept | learns at arm: the booked op's arm is refused EDENIED | yes, that slot, exactly as master loses a BLOCKED tracked request |
| booking cannot be placed (no free session, too near, no anchor) | `radiant_radio_book()` returns EBUSY/EDENIED | today's plan: the chunk stops a full lead before the op | no |
| scan's extension refused at the booking (F2 layout A) | EXTEND_FAILED, chunk ABORTED | partial credit, as ADR 0013 | no |
| scan's own request BLOCKED (short notice, below) | DENIED terminal for the chunk; the elastic lead grows | scan keeps its request, waits for the next pass | no (scan only) |
| booked op aborted before its grant (a rebuild, a replan) | session reverts to the booking, window kept | the rebuilt op re-matches the booking | no |
| booked op never arms (channel closed, slot moved) | START with nothing staged is handed back at once (`orphan`) | nothing | no; costs the other stack the booked air once |
| booking START arrives before the op is staged | handed back (`book_early`) | the arm then finds no booking, too near: DENIED | yes; expected 0, counted |
| ETIME from `program_rx()` in the booked grant | as today (FAILED after the grant) | as today | yes, as today |

The design point is that no refusal on the **scan** side can reach a tracked
slot, because the tracked slot's request was placed in another session before
the scan could hold its air. That is the property (a) lacked under Thread.

### Scan placements: a separate, adaptive lead

The scan chunk after a tracked slot, and the chunk after a `max_window_us`
boundary, are fresh requests in a free session. Nothing can book them a lead
ahead without a third session (the operation in front of them is short). So
their notice is a separate figure, `caps.min_arm_lead_elastic_us`:

- It starts at `CONFIG_RADIANT_GATE_MPSL_D2_SCAN_LEAD_US` (3000 us; the note
  first said 2000, from F3's 50 us `ACTION_REQUEST` result, and the bench showed
  a fresh request needs 2 ms: see What changed once it was on air).
- An elastic request BLOCKED although it had been given that notice doubles
  it, up to the full `PLACE_LEAD_US`; each elastic grant takes 250 us back off
  it. The gate writes the live value into
  the caps struct, which the scheduler re-reads every pass. This is a safety
  backoff, not candidate (e): it exists so a stack that refuses short notice
  (Thread, bug 26) does not turn the scan into a request storm.
- It applies only to scan chunks and energy-detect sweeps, marked
  `RADIANT_RX_ELASTIC`. Tracked windows and transmits keep the full lead
  unless booked.

Under a stack that refuses short notice the after-slot and mid-period bands
therefore come back, at whatever lead the backoff settles on. A third session
would close the after-slot band there too; the plan allows one more session,
so that is recorded as an option, not built.

### R1: two tracked channels 2.6-24.4 ms apart

Both keep their slots, with two sessions, by the same booking:

- **No search running.** The committed-early operation A holds one session;
  the scheduler books B into the other. When A completes, B is armed into its
  booking, and A's next slot is booked into the session A just freed. Both
  slots are fresh requests with ~245 ms of notice. Nothing is listening in
  between, so nothing is lost.
- **A search running.** The scan holds one session and A's booking the other,
  so B cannot be booked until a session frees. The scheduler therefore stops
  the scan at **B − lead** (not A − gap) whenever B could not otherwise be
  booked in time; at that instant the scan's session is free, A is armed into
  its booking, and B is booked with a full lead. The cost is listening from
  B − lead to A (lead − offset, 0-22 ms), only in periods with an R1 pair and a
  search.

### Session counts and RAM

`MPSL_TIMESLOT_SESSION_COUNT`: `gate.conf` and `coex.conf` 1 -> 2,
`thread.conf` 2 -> 3, `matter.conf` 3 -> 4 (one more each; the RRAM flash
driver's invisible session stays). If the second `session_open` fails, the gate
logs it and runs single-session with booking off (`caps.booking` false), so an
app that does not raise its count (hrm_ble, treadmill) behaves as master.

### Zero cost

All core code is under `CONFIG_RADIANT_SCHED_BOOKING`, selected by
`CONFIG_RADIANT_GATE_MPSL_D2` (default y with `RADIANT_BACKEND_NRF_GATE_MPSL`).
The caps fields, `RADIANT_RX_ELASTIC` and `radiant_radio_book()` exist only in
builds with it. Plain builds must be byte-identical
(`scripts/check_zero_cost.ps1 -BaseRef 237b7c4`).

### Counters

A 5 s `D2STAT` log line (`CONFIG_RADIANT_GATE_MPSL_D2_STATS_LOG`,
`d2_stats.conf`, bench only): per session placed/granted/blocked; extensions
ok/failed; bookings placed/hit/denied/blocked/mismatched/orphaned/early; the
elastic lead now/min/max and elastic blocks; the denial sources; `near`.


### What changed once it was on air

The note above is the design as first written. Seven defects or wrong
assumptions turned up after it: the first in the scheduler ztest, the other six
only on air, on `gate.conf` with no other stack and then under Thread, where
neither the fake radio nor the fake arbiter could show them. Each is
fixed on the branch (commits 7b2b0ae, 78ce810, 946a6a5, 79ce02f) and measured
in the raw logs (`s1_rule1` ... `s14_rule1`, `thread1` ... `thread4_skip`).

1. **The scheduler's booking table aged out one entry too late** (+1 ms grace
   after the booked close). An R1 pair's second booking found the table full
   every other period: the ztest R1 case lost 6 of 12 later slots until it was
   pruned at the close. (ztest, first run; fixed before any R1 air.)
2. **A fresh `mpsl_timeslot_request()` with under 2 ms of notice is refused
   even with no other stack.** Phase 0c F3 measured `ACTION_REQUEST` at a
   grant's end, which MPSL grants at 50 us; a request from thread context is
   different. Measured on `gate.conf` (`D2EL` bins): every elastic request
   that reached MPSL with < 2 ms of notice was refused (424/424 and 448/448 in
   two readings), and 0 of 970 with >= 2 ms. The starting scan lead is
   therefore 3000 us, not 2000.
3. **The elastic backoff reacted to our own lateness.** The refusals in (2)
   were requests deferred by `-NRF_EAGAIN` until their session went idle;
   doubling the lead on them drove it to 12.5 ms for nothing. The lead now
   grows only when a refused request had been given the elastic notice.
4. **With two sessions MPSL judges a new request against the other session's
   whole booked timeslot at once**, not the air the core still wants. The scan
   chunk acquired inside a tracked window's completion overlapped that window's
   follow-on reserve and was refused (51/51 refusals at 2-8 ms notice). The
   gate now holds such a placement until the running timeslot ends (`dfl`),
   the wait a single session gets from `-NRF_EAGAIN`.
5. **A tracked window that received a frame holds its timeslot to the grant's
   own end.** With a frame, the backend's owed release never fires early:
   every such window ended at its backstop compare, 5.04 ms after START
   (`t0fix bs` = frames received, 120/120, 265/265, 322/322; empty windows
   released at ~0.8 ms). So the chunk after it waited ~4.3 ms and then had too
   little notice. The scheduler now opens the post-slot chunk
   `RADIANT_SCHED_BOOK_TAIL_US` (2 ms) after close + follow-on, and the gate
   refuses locally (`late_drop`) an elastic request whose notice ran out while
   held, instead of letting MPSL refuse it. (The early release itself was not
   root-caused: see Unexplained. It is master's code path and costs master the
   same air.)
6. **Bookings and scans fought for the idle session.** Booking first put the
   scan in the session still being released. A far committed operation is now
   booked after the chunk in front of it is armed (it cannot be reached before
   that booking is placed), so the chunk gets the idle session; both callers
   prefer the idle one.
7. **Under Thread, MPSL asserted (`69:108`) right after an `EXTEND_FAILED`**,
   in both of the first two d2 Thread runs (see the MPSL assert section). After
   three refusals in a row the gate now stops asking for extensions for 64
   elastic grants and ends each at its first compare exactly as the refusal
   would have (same instant, ABORTED). With that, two further Thread runs of
   9000 placements each had no assert.

Per tracked channel the prediction is the gap before it (book lead 1.2 ms +
dispatch 0.2 + `BOOK_GAP` 3.0 + frame tail, about 4.5 ms), the window (0.4 ms),
and the gap after it (2 ms of `BOOK_TAIL` past close + follow-on, then the

test's ceiling is `n x (window + 4.5 + 3.4 + 3.0 ms) + 3 x (3.4 + 1.5 ms)`.
Every tracked slot is booked, none is missed or denied, no booking is placed
into held elastic air. The bands stay phase-locked (`moves=0`), but none is
wider than 8.4 ms. What remains at 8 channels (27 %) is two placement-free
gaps of ~8 ms per channel: d2 removes the 24 ms of notice, not the
per-channel turnaround.

**On air, `gate.conf`** (phase bins from `TEMPDIAG`, 10 ms wide, so coarse):
every non-zero mask on d2 read `0x00ffffff` or `0x01ffffff` (24-25 of 25 bins,
21 and 5 of 28 reports in the discovery leg) against master's locked
`0x003fcffc` (20 of 25; 38 of 45). In-band discovery: d2 4 of 5, master 1 of 5

slot, because the slot no longer resets the chunk train. The scan's duty is
still low: under Thread the elastic lead backs off to 16-24 ms (MPSL refused
elastic requests given >= 8 ms of notice, 77-163 per run), and every chunk is
one 10 ms first bite. So under Thread d2 listens ~25-30 % of the time with
every phase covered within a few seconds, where master listens ~25 % with

completed trials (131-132 and 236 ms phases, found at 18-21 s), **master 0 of
6**. Both d2 misses were the 4.6 ms phase, inside the after-slot band.

**On air, under BLE** (1 s connectable advertiser): d2 heard **every bin** in both

doubles on those refusals (to 24 ms at times, `el [3000..24000]`) and then
decays. It costs scan duty, not tracked slots; a lead that grows only on
refusals that correlate with short notice would fix it.

### R1: two tracked channels 2.6-24.4 ms apart

| Case | Master | (d2) |
|---|---|---|
| ztest, no search, 1/5/12/24 ms | later slot lost 12/12 at 5, 12, 24 ms | **both kept 12/12 at every offset** |
| ztest, a search running, 1/5/12/24 ms | (lost, as above) | **both kept 12/12**; the chunk is cut a lead before B at 5 and 12 ms (`book_r1_cut` 53) |
| on air, R1 walk (B acquired at +40 ms, walked to +12.5 ms, 60 s window) | **A 221 (9.3 % lost), B 0 (100 %)**; reference on B 0.00 % | **A 217 (11.0 %), B 218 (10.6 %)**; reference on B 0.00 % |

**On air R1 is fixed.** The pair gets two slots per period (435 frames in the
window) where master gets one (221). The ~10 % both channels lose matches the
DUT's single-channel loss in this room in the same hour (5-10 % on `gate.conf`
against a Garmin at 0-0.8 %), and the gate counted every slot booked and
granted (`hi 2869/2869/0/2868`, `early 0`). One run per image (A/B, not
A/B/A), but 218 frames against 0 is not a room effect.

How: each tracked slot is booked about a period ahead, in the session the
other slot's grant has just freed, so neither has to be placed inside the
other's lead. With a search running, the chunk in front of A is stopped one
lead before B (rather than 4.5 ms before A), so that B can still be booked when
the chunk's session frees. That costs listening between B minus a lead and A,
only in periods that have an R1 pair and a search.

| Path | What happens | Slot lost? |
|---|---|---|
| Booking BLOCKED (answered at placement) | session marked failed; the booked operation's arm is refused EDENIED | yes, that slot, as master loses a BLOCKED tracked request (ztest; on air `blk=0` in every run) |

**Longest time an armed operation can go without a terminal:** unchanged. No
operation is longer than `max_window_us` (94.2 ms); a booked operation's

placed or late-dropped with a DENIED terminal; the deadline backstop (start
minus now plus 20 ms) still covers a request MPSL never answers. All far under
the A9-3 reclaim (100 ms past an operation's end).

## Composition

- **With (e)** (per-stack lead): d2 keeps the full `PLACE_LEAD_US` only for
  bookings, which are placed a period ahead, so the lead stops costing
  listening around tracked slots. What (e) would still tune is the elastic
  lead: under Thread d2's backoff settles at 16-24 ms, and an (e) sweep would
  say whether that is what Thread needs or only where the backoff stops.
- **With (a)**: alternatives at the tracked boundary, as (a) said (a bridging
  extension over a booked instant is F2 layout B). (a)'s held grant could still
  serve scan-to-scan boundaries where extensions are granted (gate, BLE);
  under Thread there is nothing to hold.
- **With (d1)**: complementary in principle (d1 chains the scan's next chunk at
  a grant's end; d2 books the committed operation). Under Thread the scan
  needed 16-24 ms of notice, so d1's short-notice chain would likely be refused
  there too.
- **A third session** would let the post-slot chunk be booked during the
  pre-slot chunk, closing the after-slot band under Thread as well. Not built.

## Unexplained

- **Why a tracked window that received a frame never releases its grant
  early.** The backend's owed release (THE GRANT HANDOFF) fires early for empty
  windows (`t0fix rel`) and never for windows that received a frame (`t0fix
  bs` equals the frames received, exactly, in every reading). `D2SETTLE` shows
  the settle path finding nothing owed and an immediate release finding the
  grant already gone; the path that clears the debt without provoking the end
  was not found. This is master's code, so master too holds every
  frame-bearing tracked window's follow-on reserve (~4 ms) to the grant's end,
  which is air the other stack could have had. d2 works around it (defect 5);
  fixing it would shrink d2's after-slot band from ~9.5 ms to ~5.5 ms.
- **MPSL ASSERT 69:108**: closed source; correlated with extension refusals
  under Thread (above), not root-caused.
- **The room**: DUT tracked loss swung from 5 % to 36 % on the same master
  image within an hour while the Garmin read 0-0.8 %.
- **BLE DUT loss**: the two d2 BLE runs (11.9 %, 18.1 %) read higher than the
  master run between them (8.8 %), with every tracked window but the
  advertiser-refused bookings granted. Granted windows that hear nothing are
  not the scheduler's loss, but whether d2's BLE timing (more grants, the
  elastic lead swinging to 24 ms) affects reception was not isolated.
